The problem

Pipes Fail At The Worst Possible Time.

Most plumbing emergencies start small. A drip under the sink, a gurgle in the tub drain, a water heater that rumbles for a week. Then the pipe lets go at 2 a.m. on a Sunday, and every minute the water runs costs you drywall, flooring and sleep.

North Brunswick homeowners need a plumber who picks up immediately, not a call center that books next Tuesday. That is the job of our 24/7 emergency line. You call, we ask a few direct questions, and we line up the right plumber for the problem you described.

If water is already moving, shut off the main valve first. It sits near the water meter or where the line enters the house. Then call us and we will help with the next step, whether that is a burst pipe, a sewer backup or a failed water heater.

Our response

One Call Covers Every Job.

Water heaters, leaks, drains, sewer lines and full repipes all come through one number, so you are never bounced between specialists. Here is how the work breaks down for most North Brunswick calls.

Emergency drain cleaning clears kitchen, tub and main-line clogs, and hydro jetting handles the roots and grease a snake cannot finish.

A failed tank starts with water heater repair. When the tank is leaking from the bottom, replacement is usually the honest answer.

Sewer line repair and camera inspection show what is wrong underground before anyone digs.

Toilet repair, faucet and sink repair and garbage disposal repair cover the fixtures that fail most often.

Planned work such as tankless water heaters, repiping and plumbing installation gets a scheduled visit and a clear price first.

Local detail

What North Brunswick Houses Ask Of Their Plumbing.

North Brunswick is a township of about 43,900 residents (2020 Census) and 15,789 housing units, stretched along Route 1 and Route 130 between New Brunswick and South Brunswick. Neighborhoods such as Livingston Park, Franklin Park, Black Horse, Red Lion and Maple Meade sit right next to newer developments, so one street can have copper from the 1960s and PEX from last year.

The water comes from the township's own utility, which Veolia operates. Its 2022 water quality report lists hardness of 67.5 ppm as calcium carbonate, about 3.9 grains per gallon. That is moderately hard, so tank water heaters collect scale slowly and an annual flush is worth doing.

New Jersey law says plumbing work must be done by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor, and licensees must show their license number on invoices and advertising. You can ask any plumber for that number and check it in the state's Division of Consumer Affairs license verification system.

Permit rules vary, so ask the North Brunswick building department before work begins. New Jersey's plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2021 edition, and a licensed plumber will know what your job needs.
