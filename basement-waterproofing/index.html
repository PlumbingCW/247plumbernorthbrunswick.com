Basement Waterproofing In North Brunswick, NJ

Water finds its way into a basement through the floor joint, cracks, window wells and a failing drain. The fix depends on which path the water takes.

Written by the North Brunswick 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated October 13, 2026

Basement waterproofing in North Brunswick, NJ starts with a question: where is the water coming from? Rain-driven seepage, a high water table, a failed gutter outlet and a leaking pipe look alike on the floor, and they call for different fixes.

We look at the whole path, from roof and grading to the sump pit and discharge. If your pump is failing or the pit floods, see sump pump repair. If you suspect a pipe, our leak detection service tells you whether the water comes from inside the walls.

Waterproofing is a planned project. If water is entering your basement right now, call the 24/7 line.

Diagnosis

Where Basement Water Comes From

Water paths and clues
SourceClueTypical fix
Surface waterWet after rain, near downspouts, grading slopes to the houseExtend downspouts, regrade, seal window wells
Floor and wall jointWater along the base of the wallInterior drain channel to a sump
Cracks in walls or slabStreaks, stains or active seepageSeal or inject the crack, check drainage
High water tableWater rises after long wet spellsPerimeter drain and sump pump
Plumbing leakWater stays after a dry weekLocate and repair the pipe

A moisture meter and a look at the exterior tell us more than a quick visual does.

Local conditions

Why This Area Floods

North Brunswick sits along Lawrence Brook and the Raritan River basin, and the township went through the flood on September 1, 2021, when Tropical Storm Ida's water damaged the municipal complex on Hermann Road. Basements throughout central New Jersey took water that night.

We do not claim any one street floods. We do say that the best waterproofing is a system: gutters, grading, drainage, a sump pump and a battery backup that all work together.

Systems

Systems We Install And Service

Typical components

  • ✓Interior perimeter drain that catches water at the floor and wall joint.
  • ✓A sump pit with a pump and a check valve.
  • ✓A battery backup or water-powered backup for outages during storms.
  • ✓A discharge line routed away from the foundation.
  • ✓Window well drains and covers.
  • ✓Gutter and downspout extensions so roof water does not land at the wall.

Exterior excavation and large-scale foundation repair are specialist work. We say so when a job needs a different contractor, and we handle the plumbing side, including the pump and discharge.

Process

How An Assessment Works

  1. 1
    Look outside first

    We check gutters, downspouts, grading and window wells.

  2. 2
    Inspect inside

    We check walls, floor joints, cracks, the sump and any floor drains.

  3. 3
    Test for plumbing leaks

    We rule out a supply or drain leak with a meter test and, if needed, a camera.

  4. 4
    Recommend a plan

    You get a short list of options from low-cost fixes to full drainage.

Protection

Protecting What You Store Down There

Lift boxes off the floor, put valuables on shelves, and keep electronics and the furnace off the slab where possible. A raised platform for a water heater or washing machine saves money after a flood.

If your basement has a floor drain, ask us about a backwater valve. It helps when sewer lines surcharge during a storm.

Pricing

What Affects A Waterproofing Price

Source of the water

Fixing a downspout costs far less than installing a perimeter drain.

Length of drain

More wall length means more channel to install.

Sump pump setup

Single pump, backup pump and a battery system each add cost.

Discharge route

A short run costs less than one that must cross a yard.

Finished surfaces

Finished walls and floors need more restoration.

Insurance, honestly

Standard homeowners policies generally exclude groundwater seepage and flood. Flood coverage is a separate policy. Sudden pipe bursts are treated differently, so ask your insurer.
