Toilet Keeps Clogging? A North Brunswick Homeowner Guide

Written by the North Brunswick 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026

One clog is bad luck. A toilet that clogs every week is telling you something. The cause may be in the toilet, in the drain beneath the floor or in the main line that serves the whole house.

This guide walks through what to check in order, so you can tell a simple fix from a job for a plumber. It is written for North Brunswick homeowners, from older houses in Livingston Park to newer developments off Route 130.

If the toilet is overflowing right now, stop reading, close the valve behind the bowl and call the 24/7 line.

Check one

Start With The Easy Causes

Most repeat clogs come from three habits. Too much toilet paper, wipes labeled flushable and small items such as cotton swabs. None of those break down quickly, and they gather at the bend inside the toilet or at the first offset in the drain.

A flange plunger, the kind with a rubber flap, seals the bowl better than a cup plunger. Use slow, firm strokes and keep the water level high enough to cover the cup. If three tries do not move the water, stop and move to the next step.

Diagnosis

Match The Symptom To The Cause

Notice the pattern before you call, and tell the dispatcher. A single-fixture problem and a whole-house problem need different tools.

Deeper causes

When The Problem Is The Vent Or The Drain

Every drain needs air. A blocked vent on the roof makes water drain slowly and leaves a gurgle behind. A vent that has frozen over in winter, or one choked with leaves, can mimic a clog.

A drain that has sagged under the slab or in a crawlspace leaves a low spot, called a belly, where paper collects. That clog returns no matter how often you plunge. A camera shows it, and a repair is the only lasting cure. Our sewer camera inspection page explains what a camera finds.

North Brunswick facts

What The Township Handles And What You Do

North Brunswick's water and sewer office is on the second floor of the municipal building at 710 Hermann Road, open from 8:30 to 4 on business days. If neighbors are having the same trouble, ask the office whether the township main is involved.

If only your house is affected, the clog is on your side of the connection, and a plumber can find it. Ask the township what you are responsible for before you pay for work in the street.

Plumber's tools

What A Plumber Does That A Plunger Cannot

A closet auger passes through the toilet's built-in trap and clears what a plunger cannot reach. A drum machine reaches farther, into the branch drain. If those fail, the toilet may need to be pulled and the flange checked.

After the clog is cleared, a camera tells you whether it was a one-off or a symptom. If the pipe is cracked, rooted or bellied, see drain cleaning and sewer line repair for the options. For a toilet that clogs because of its design, toilet repair and replacement are covered on their own page.

Prevention

Habits That Keep The Line Clear

Simple habits

  • ✓Flush only toilet paper and human waste.
  • ✓Put wipes, floss and cotton swabs in the trash.
  • ✓Use less paper per flush rather than one huge wad.
  • ✓Keep a flange plunger in each bathroom.
  • ✓Tell your household where the shut-off valve is.

These steps help any house. They matter more in older homes with long horizontal drain runs. If you live near the Milltown or East Brunswick line, our Milltown and East Brunswick pages show that we cover those areas too.

Households

Kids, Guests And Rentals

Households with small children see toys, wipes and extra paper go down the toilet. Short, friendly house rules help, and so does a sign above the bowl in a rental or guest bath.

Landlords can add a small trash can with a lid next to the toilet. It gives guests a better place for things that should not be flushed and cuts service calls. Under the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, which New Jersey adopts, each fixture needs a trap and a vent, which is why a venting problem can look like a clog.

Urgent

When To Call Right Away

Call at once if sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain, if more than one toilet is affected or if the water level in the bowl rises when you run the washer. Those point to the main line.

A clog that returns within days after clearing is also a call. Pouring chemical opener into a toilet makes it worse, because the liquid sits in the bowl and can splash on the person who works on it next.
