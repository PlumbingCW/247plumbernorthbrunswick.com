How To Install A Sump Pump: A North Brunswick Homeowner Guide

Written by the North Brunswick 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026

A sump pump is a simple machine in a not-so-simple setting. The pit, the float, the check valve and the discharge all have to work together, and a mistake shows up in the worst storm.

This guide explains how an installation works, so you can judge a quote or decide how much you want to do yourself.

If your pit is filling now and the pump is dead, check the plug and the GFCI first, then call the 24/7 line.

Parts

The Parts And What Each One Does

Sump system parts
PartJobCommon mistake
Pit and linerCollects water below the floorToo small, causing short cycling
PumpMoves water out of the pitUndersized for the lift
Float switchStarts the pump at a set levelStuck against the wall of the pit
Check valveStops water falling backMissing or installed backward
Discharge pipeCarries water outsideEnds too close to the house
Backup powerKeeps the pit dry in an outageSkipped entirely
Process

Step By Step

  1. 1
    Locate and cut the pit

    A pit goes at the lowest point where water collects. Cutting concrete is dusty, heavy work.

  2. 2
    Set the liner and gravel

    The liner sits level on a gravel bed, and the slab is patched around it.

  3. 3
    Place the pump

    The pump sits on a flat base and the float swings free.

  4. 4
    Add the check valve and discharge

    The pipe rises, passes through the rim joist and exits at least several feet from the foundation.

  5. 5
    Power and test

    A dedicated GFCI outlet feeds the pump. Fill the pit with a hose and watch a full cycle.

Cutting the slab and routing the discharge are the hard parts. Many homeowners buy the pump and hire the rest. Our sump pump repair page shows what a plumber checks.

Discharge

Where The Water Should Go

Discharge should drain away from the house onto ground that slopes down. Do not connect it to the sanitary sewer, since the township's sewer is not meant for stormwater. Ask the North Brunswick building department what local rules apply to your lot.

In winter, a discharge that ends in the open can freeze. A short drop with a free-flowing outlet helps, and a screen keeps animals out.

North Brunswick facts

Permits, Mark-Outs And Ida

Before digging outside, call New Jersey 811 so buried utilities are marked. Ask the building department whether a permit or inspection applies to a new pit or electrical circuit.

The reminder for planning comes from the township's own history. Tropical Storm Ida's floodwater on September 1, 2021 knocked out electrical service at the municipal complex on Hermann Road. A battery backup keeps a pump running when the grid does not.

Pump types

Choosing Between Submersible And Pedestal Pumps

A submersible pump sits in the pit and runs quietly. It handles heavy use well and does not take up floor space. A pedestal pump sits above the pit on a column, costs less and is easier to service, though it is louder and has a shorter life in heavy use.

For a pit that sees regular water, most plumbers lean toward submersible units. For a pit that rarely runs, a pedestal pump may do. Ask about the float type too, since a tethered float needs more room to swing than a vertical float.

Sizing

Sizing The Pump To The Job

Two numbers decide the size. How much water has to move, and how high it has to lift it to the discharge point. A pump moving water up a tall wall and across a long yard needs more power than one shooting it out a nearby window well.

An undersized pump runs constantly and wears out. An oversized pump short cycles and wears out too. A plumber who asks about both is doing the work right.

Testing

Testing Your New Pump

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float rise. The pump should start, drain the pit and shut off without chattering. Check the discharge outside and make sure water flows away from the house.

Repeat the test every few months. A pump that sits idle for a year can seize, and a quick test is the cheapest insurance you can buy. The install falls under the state plumbing and electrical subcodes, and the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, is the plumbing reference.

Hire

When To Hire Instead

Hire a plumber if the slab must be cut, if the discharge crosses a driveway, if no outlet exists or if the basement already floods. See basement waterproofing for larger systems.

Homeowners in Milltown near Lawrence Brook and in East Brunswick near Farrington Lake often have the lowest basements, so plan carefully.
