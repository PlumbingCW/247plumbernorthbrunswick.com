Emergency Sump Pump Repair in North Brunswick, NJ

A sump pump that fails during a storm turns a damp basement into a flooded one. We repair, replace and back up pumps so they work when you need them.

When the pit is filling and the pump will not run, call for emergency sump pump repair in North Brunswick, NJ. The 24/7 line is open, and we offer same-day appointments when available.

Check that the pump is plugged in and the GFCI outlet is not tripped. Look at the float. If it is stuck, lifting it by hand may start the pump. Keep electronics and boxes off the floor, and never step into standing water if the outlet may be wet.

A pump is one part of a drainage system. If water keeps coming in, see basement waterproofing. If the discharge line backs up into a floor drain, see sewer line cleaning.

Failure

Why Pumps Fail

Pump failure modes
SymptomLikely causeFix
Pump will not startNo power, tripped GFCI, stuck floatReset, free the float, test
Runs but does not move waterClogged intake or failed check valveClean the intake, replace the valve
Runs constantlyStuck float or high water tableAdjust the float, check the discharge
Short cyclesCheck valve failure or pit too smallReplace the valve, resize the pit
Loud or grindingWorn bearings or debrisReplace the pump
Fails in power outageNo backupAdd battery backup

Most pumps last several years, depending on use. A pump that runs hard each storm wears faster.

Warning signs

Symptoms Your Pump Needs Attention

Warning signs

  • ✓The pump has not been tested in over a year.
  • ✓You hear the motor run but the pit does not drain.
  • ✓The pit holds standing water in dry weather.
  • ✓Rust or sediment collects on the pump.
  • ✓The discharge pipe drips or floods near the foundation.

Test it by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should kick on, clear it and shut off.

Backup

Backup Power And Redundancy

Storms that flood basements also cause power outages. A battery backup or a water-powered backup keeps the pit empty when the main pump has no power. A second pump set slightly higher gives redundancy if the first fails.

The township learned this the hard way on September 1, 2021, when Tropical Storm Ida's floodwater took out electrical service and boilers at the municipal complex on Hermann Road. A power-dependent system is only as reliable as the grid.

Install

Installing Or Replacing A Pump

  1. 1
    Check the pit

    We inspect the pit size, the inlet, the float and the discharge route.

  2. 2
    Choose the pump

    We size the pump to the water volume and the lift to the discharge point.

  3. 3
    Install with a check valve

    A check valve keeps water from falling back into the pit.

  4. 4
    Test under load

    We fill the pit, run the pump through a cycle and check the discharge outside.

Discharge should go well away from the foundation and not into the sanitary sewer. Ask the North Brunswick building department what rules apply at your address.

Maintenance

Keeping A Pump Ready

Test the pump every few months and before a forecast storm. Clear the pit of debris. Make sure the discharge pipe is open and sloped away from the house. In winter, make sure the outlet does not freeze shut.

Pricing

What Affects Sump Pump Pricing

Pump type

Submersible and pedestal pumps differ in cost and lifespan.

Backup system

Battery and water-powered backups add cost.

Pit condition

A new pit or liner adds work.

Discharge route

A long or buried line adds pipe and labor.

Hour of the visit

A storm-night call costs more than a daytime appointment.

Insurance, honestly

Many standard policies do not cover water that backs up or seeps in unless you add a water backup endorsement. Ask your insurer before storm season.
