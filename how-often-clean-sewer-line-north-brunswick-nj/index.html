How Often Should Sewer Lines Be Cleaned Out In North Brunswick?

  • 24/7 dispatch

Written by the North Brunswick 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026

Ask ten plumbers how often a sewer line needs cleaning and you will hear ten answers. The honest one is that it depends on the pipe, the trees, the kitchen and the household.

This guide gives you the factors that set the interval and the signs that say it is time. It also explains why a camera visit tells you more than a calendar.

If sewage is backing up now, stop using water and call the 24/7 line.

Interval

Why There Is No Universal Schedule

A newer plastic line with no trees nearby may never need cleaning. A clay line with willow roots at every joint may need it every year or two. A restaurant line may need monthly service.

Four things decide the answer: the pipe material, the age and condition of the joints, what goes down the drain and the trees near the lateral.

Signs

Warning Signs

Clean the line sooner if you notice

  • ✓Slow drains in several fixtures at once.
  • ✓Gurgling when the washer drains.
  • ✓A sewer smell near the cleanout or in the yard.
  • ✓A floor drain that rises during heavy laundry.
  • ✓A lush strip of lawn above the lateral.

Any of these deserves a camera visit. See sewer line cleaning and sewer inspection for what happens.

North Brunswick facts

Know Your Township's Role

North Brunswick's water and sewer office sits on the second floor of the municipal building at 710 Hermann Road and is open 8:30 to 4. The township's side of the system ends at a point your deed or the office can explain.

The township's 2020 housing count was 15,789 units, up from 13,932 in 2000. Older neighborhoods such as Livingston Park and Black Horse sit beside newer builds, so one street can have very different laterals.

Additives

Do Not Rely On Additives

Enzyme and chemical treatments marketed for sewer lines do little for roots and nothing for a collapsed pipe. They can help with light grease, but they are no substitute for mechanical cleaning when a line is narrowing.

If you want a preventive plan, ask for camera footage at each visit. The video shows whether intervals should shrink. Our hydro jetting page explains the next step up when cabling is not enough.

Roots

What Roots Look Like On Camera

Roots on a camera look like fine white hair or a thick brown mat entering at a joint. Fine roots grow fast but cut easily. Thick roots mean a longer-standing problem and more risk to the pipe.

Seeing the roots tells you whether cleaning will hold for a year or only a season. It also tells you if lining or repair is worth considering before the next backup.

Use

How Laundry And Kitchens Change The Answer

A home with a heavy laundry load sends lint and soap into the line daily. A home with a busy kitchen sends grease. Both shorten the interval. A household of two with a small laundry load can often go years between cleanings.

Think about your use, not just your pipe. If your habits change, with a new baby, a basement apartment or a home business, tell your plumber and revisit the schedule.

Booking

Questions To Ask When You Book A Cleaning

Ask whether the visit includes a camera, whether you get the footage and what method will be used. Ask what happens if the cable cannot pass a blockage, and what the follow-up looks like.

A plumber who answers clearly is more likely to give you a clean line and a useful report. A plumber who avoids the questions deserves another quote. Plumbing codes generally call for cleanouts at set points on a building drain, and New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, so ask the building department what your house should have.

Real estate

Selling Or Buying

A camera inspection before a sale protects both sides. Buyers in Milltown and South Brunswick often ask for it on older homes. The footage may help negotiate a repair credit.
