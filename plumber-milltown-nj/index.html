A small borough has a particular plumbing story, and Milltown's runs through its brook and its factories.

The brook

A Mill Town On Lawrence Brook

Lawrence Brook runs through the center of Milltown, and a dam on Main Street forms Mill Pond. The borough covers 1.59 square miles and counted 7,037 residents in 2020, living in about 2,707 housing units.

Homes close to the brook sit on low ground, and basements there are the first to feel a wet season. A reliable pump, a clear discharge and a battery backup are the best protection. See our sump pump repair page for how those parts fit together. We do not name streets as flood-prone, but we do ask where the pit sits and how the pump is wired.

Mill heritage

Michelin, India Rubber And The Houses That Grew Around Them

Milltown grew around its rubber factories. The Michelin plant employed about 3,000 people at its peak, and the former India Rubber factory has been converted into condominiums. The borough's housing includes workers' homes from the factory era alongside later homes and the converted mill.

Older workers' houses often have original galvanized supply lines and clay or cast iron drains. When one fails, a camera inspection shows what is wrong, and a repiping plan can follow when leaks keep returning. Condo owners in converted buildings should check with their association about shared lines before calling.

Before We Arrive

Do these first while you wait, since water damage grows with every minute.

Close the main

Find the main valve where the line enters the house and close it fully.

Move belongings

Lift items off the floor, especially in a basement near the brook.

Avoid electrical risk

Stay clear of wet outlets and panels.

Call neighbors

In attached homes, a leak can reach next door.

Roads

Getting Around: Ryders Lane And The Turnpike

Ryders Lane, County Route 617, is a main road through the borough, and the New Jersey Turnpike passes about a mile away. We never promise an arrival time, but we do tell you honestly when we expect to reach you.

Small lots

Condos, Rowhouses And Small Lots

Many Milltown properties are on small lots with short runs between the house and the street. That keeps service lines short, but it also means a root, a leak or a frozen line is never far from the foundation. If you share walls with a neighbor, tell us, because a leak can travel next door.

For condo owners, the association usually owns the main stacks and the exterior lines. Ask your management who handles a given pipe before calling. If the problem is inside your unit, the fixture, the shut-off and the branch line are yours, and we can help.
