How To Plumb A Bathtub: A North Brunswick Homeowner Guide

Written by the North Brunswick 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026

A bathtub has three plumbing systems: the supply that fills it, the drain that empties it and the vent that lets the drain breathe. Each follows a code, and the order of work matters.

This guide explains how a tub is plumbed so you can plan a remodel, read a quote and ask good questions.

Plumbing work in New Jersey must be done by a licensed professional, and permits and inspections apply to new installs.

Basics

The Three Systems

Tub plumbing at a glance
SystemPartsWhat it does
SupplyHot and cold lines, valve, spoutBrings water to the tub
DrainShoe, overflow, tailpiece, P-trapTakes water away and blocks sewer gas
VentPipe tied to the main vent stackLets air in so the trap does not siphon

A tub drain is usually 1.5 inches in diameter and must slope toward the main drain. The trap sits below the floor, so access from below or through a panel is important.

Process

Order Of Work

  1. 1
    Frame and set height

    Locate the tub, drain and valve before walls close.

  2. 2
    Rough-in drain and vent

    Set the trap, connect it to the branch drain and tie in the vent.

  3. 3
    Rough-in supply

    Run hot and cold lines to the valve location, with stops where the code requires.

  4. 4
    Pressure and leak test

    Test supply lines and fill the drain with water before the walls close.

  5. 5
    Set the tub and finish

    Install the tub, connect the overflow, seal and then finish the wall and trim.

Testing before closing the wall is the step most often skipped, and the one that saves the most money. Our plumbing installation page describes how we approach it.

Valves

Valves, Anti-Scald And Pressure

A modern tub valve balances hot and cold pressure so the water does not spike when someone flushes a toilet. Local code often requires this protection. Ask your plumber which valve fits your fixture.

A water heater set too high raises scald risk. Many plumbers recommend a setting that balances comfort with safety. See water heater installation for related details.

North Brunswick facts

Licensing And Code In New Jersey

New Jersey law, N.J.S.A. 45:14C-1, says plumbing work is done by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. Licensees must display their plumbing license number on vehicles, invoices and advertising.

The plumbing subcode adopted by the state is the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition. Ask the North Brunswick building department what permit and inspection your tub project needs. The township counted 15,789 housing units in 2020, many with older tubs ready for replacement.

Choosing

Choosing A Tub And Matching The Rough-In

Tub sizes, drain positions and overflow styles vary. A left-hand drain tub in a right-hand alcove will not line up. Measure the alcove, check the drain position in the spec sheet and match the valve height to the wall tile.

Cast iron and steel tubs are heavy and need a solid floor. Acrylic tubs flex and need full support underneath. A mortar or foam bed under an acrylic tub keeps it from creaking and cracking.

Access

Access Panels And Why They Matter

Leave an access panel to the valve and, if you can, to the trap. Water supply valves wear out, and a plumber working through a hole in the ceiling below costs more than one working through a panel.

A neat panel in the wall behind the tub or in the adjoining room stays hidden under paint and saves a wall later.

Testing

Testing Before You Close The Wall

Fill the tub and let it drain while you watch the trap and the pipes below. Run the valve with the spout off to check for leaks at the connections. Leave the supply lines pressurized for a while and watch the gauge.

A test costs minutes. A leak behind tile costs a wall. This is the single best habit for any plumbing project.

Surprises

Common Remodel Surprises

Opening a bathroom wall often reveals old galvanized supply, a notched joist or a drain with the wrong slope. Plan time and budget for one surprise.

A plumber who has seen many tubs spots the problem on the first walk-through and tells you what to expect.

Troubleshooting

Common Problems After A Tub Install

A slow drain usually means the trap or tailpiece has hair or a poor slope. A gurgle means the vent is not doing its job. A stain on the ceiling below the tub points to a leak at the overflow gasket or the drain shoe. See leak detection if you cannot find it.
