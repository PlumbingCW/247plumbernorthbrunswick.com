Can I Repipe My Own House? A North Brunswick Guide

Written by the North Brunswick 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026

Repiping looks like a weekend job on a video. It rarely is. It touches every fixture, every wall and the main shut-off, and mistakes show up as leaks inside finished drywall.

This guide explains what repiping involves and what to ask before deciding how much to do yourself.

In New Jersey, the first question is legal, not mechanical.

Licensing

What The Law Says

N.J.S.A. 45:14C-1 says plumbing is performed only by a master plumber or an authorized plumbing contractor. Licensees must show a plumbing license number on vehicles, invoices and advertising. Ask the North Brunswick building department whether homeowner permits exist for any part of your project.

Even where permits are possible, inspectors expect pressure tests, correct materials and code-compliant fittings.

Scope

What A Repipe Involves

  1. 1
    Map the system

    Every branch, riser and fixture is mapped and valved.

  2. 2
    Choose the material

    Copper, PEX or CPVC, each with its own tools and rules.

  3. 3
    Open the walls

    Access holes at each fixture and at every vertical run.

  4. 4
    Run, connect and test

    New lines are installed, pressure-tested and tied into the main.

  5. 5
    Patch and finish

    Drywall, paint and trim restore the walls.

A full house often takes a licensed crew days. Our repiping page shows how that process runs.

North Brunswick facts

North Brunswick Housing And Pipes

The township counted 15,789 housing units in 2020 on 12.31 square miles. Many homes date from the growth decades, and some still have original galvanized steel or early copper.

The township's 2022 water quality report found 0 of 31 samples above the lead action level and 0 of 31 above the copper action level. That describes the utility's tested samples. Inside your home, old solder and brass are separate questions, which an inspection answers.

Hiring

Questions To Ask A Licensed Plumber

Before you hire

  • ✓Can I see your plumbing license number and its expiration date?
  • ✓Which pipe material do you recommend for this house and why?
  • ✓How will you keep water on during the work?
  • ✓Who handles permits and inspections?
  • ✓Who patches drywall, and is it in the quote?

A confident plumber answers these without hesitation.

Partial

Signs You Might Only Need A Partial Repipe

If leaks are in one area, such as a basement run of old galvanized pipe, you may only need that run replaced. If the rest tests well, a partial job saves money and leaves finished walls alone.

A pressure test and a look at the pipe type in each zone of the house tell us. The test is simple and the answer is clear.

Paperwork

Insurance, Resale And Paperwork

Unpermitted plumbing can complicate a sale, because a buyer's inspector may ask for permits and final sign-offs. It can also complicate an insurance claim if a DIY joint fails.

Hiring a licensed plumber who pulls the permit gives you a paper trail. Keep the invoice, the permit and the inspection sign-off with your house records.

Effort

Tools, Time And The Real Learning Curve

Even a skilled DIYer needs pipe cutters, a crimp or expansion tool, a torch for copper and a way to test pressure. Those tools cost money, and the learning curve costs time. A first-time repipe often takes many weekends, with the water off for much of that time.

A professional crew works in parallel and keeps the house livable. When you add the cost of tools, mistakes and a second trip for a leak, the gap between DIY and hired narrows quickly. Add the value of your own weekends, and the licensed crew starts to look like the sensible choice for a job this size.

The state plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated, 2021 edition, and an inspector will check your work against it.

Options

A Middle Path For Homeowners

You can do prep work that saves money, such as clearing access, moving furniture and patching walls after the plumber is done. You can also learn where your shut-offs are and label them.

For partial work, such as replacing one corroded section, ask about a partial repipe. See burst pipe repair for emergencies, and plumbing installation for new lines. We serve North Brunswick, Edison and East Brunswick.
