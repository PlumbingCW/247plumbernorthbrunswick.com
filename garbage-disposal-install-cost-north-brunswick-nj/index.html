Garbage Disposal Install Cost: A North Brunswick Price Guide

Written by the North Brunswick 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 13, 2026

People search for a number, and any plumber who quotes one without seeing your sink is guessing. What can be explained is what pushes the price up or down.

This guide lists those factors, in order of how much they matter, so you can compare quotes fairly.

We do not post dollar figures on this site. You get a clear price from a plumber before work starts.

Scope

Replacement Versus First-Time Install

Replacing an old disposal with a similar one is the fastest case. A first-time install needs a sink flange, a drain cut, a power source and a dishwasher connection if you have one.

If the old unit simply jammed, a repair may avoid a purchase. See garbage disposal repair for the diagnosis. If the sink backs up even with a working disposal, the line may be partly blocked and drain cleaning comes first.

Choosing

Picking A Unit That Fits

Continuous-feed units run while you feed them. Batch-feed units run when the stopper is locked. Both work. Horsepower matters for hard waste, but a bigger motor does not make up for a weak drain.

Check the mount type, the weight and the noise rating. A sound-insulated unit costs more and is quieter. Your plumber can show options that fit the cabinet.

North Brunswick facts

Use The License As A Pricing Check

New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a master plumber or authorized plumbing contractor, and licensees must show the plumbing license number on invoices and advertising (N.J.S.A. 45:14C-1). When you compare quotes, check that each includes the license number.

A quote with no license number may be cheaper, but it is not comparable. You can verify a license through the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs system.

Features

Features That Add To The Bill

A basic disposal grinds and drains. Higher-end units add sound insulation, stainless parts and larger motors. Some have a built-in cord kit, while others need you to buy one.

Think about what you will use. A small household that scrapes plates seldom needs a heavy-duty unit. A large family that cooks daily may. Do not pay for features you will not feel.

Alternatives

When A Disposal Is Not The Right Answer

Homes on septic systems may not suit a disposal because the extra solids load the tank. Kitchens with old, narrow or flat drains may clog more with one. A compost pail often beats a disposal for vegetable scraps.

A plumber who says you do not need one is a plumber worth keeping. We will tell you if your drain cannot handle it.

Value

Warranty, Parts And Installer Experience

Disposal warranties vary from a year to many years, and some cover only the motor. Read what is covered and who pays for labor if the unit fails. A longer warranty only helps if you can use it.

Experience matters too. An installer who has done hundreds knows how to seat the mount, align the drain and avoid leaks. That skill rarely shows up in a quote, but you notice it when the first month passes without a drip.

Cleanup

Planning For Removal, Haul-Away And Cleanup

Ask whether the quote includes removing the old disposal and hauling it away. A cabinet under the sink is often cramped, so a careful installer lays down protection and wipes up afterward.

Clear the cabinet before the plumber arrives and keep pets out of the room. A clean workspace means a quicker visit and a better result.
